Traditional Hybrid: a standard Transformer, made smaller, faster and smarter with what this research proved

Research concept and direction by Adam Barnett. This plan takes a conventional decoder-only language model, the kind every lab trains, and grafts onto it only the ideas from this research that have measured evidence behind them. Each idea moves a job out of the weights (facts into memory, arithmetic into tools, skills into adapters, reasoning into checked practice), so the network can stay small and spend its capacity on understanding language and composing answers. Ideas that worked only for tiny models, or not at all, are listed at the end and left out.

Status plan, not started Target core 100M-350M parameters Hardware one RTX 3090, 64 GB Companion BRAIN_DESIGN.html

1. The model in one picture

Everything in the picture except the core is either deterministic (memory, tools, checks) or small (copy head, gate, adapters). The core is an ordinary Transformer, trained the ordinary way but on better-chosen data, so the plan can reuse standard tooling.

2. The six ideas kept, and why

Idea 1. Knowledge in a retrieval memory, used through a copy head

What we measured
Answers to stored questions: the network alone 0.13-0.24 word F1; with the retrieved exchange in the window and a learned copy head, 0.89 F1 and 56-63% word-exact (5M core, two seeds). Facts added after training are answered at once (0.86-0.88 F1). The copy head alone lifted answers from 0.13 to 0.48 the day it was added.
Design
A search index over passages and question-answer pairs; the best matches placed in the context ahead of the question; a pointer head (attention from the current state to the retrieved tokens, predicting the token that follows the attended one) mixed with the network's own distribution by a small gate that reads the state and the pointer's confidence. Retrieval by keyword (as now) plus a dense key trained on in-domain question pairs.
Why it makes the model smaller
A conventional model stores every fact in its weights, which is why it must be large. Here the weights only need to read and compose; facts live in an index that costs memory, not compute. Published retrieval models (RETRO, Atlas) match models 10-25 times their size on knowledge tasks.
Also gives
Instant updates (add a passage), deletion (remove it), and answers that can name their source.

Idea 2. Pre-train the core to use evidence, on data chosen for its job

What we measured
Same 5M model, better data: conversation error 2.02 → 1.30 bits per byte (-36%), Wikipedia 2.06 → 1.68; a 19M core on the old data did no better than 4.7M. Evidence-pair training units raised the core's use of a related passage from 23% to 35%; at 50M, prediction with evidence in front fell to 0.81 bits per byte from 1.18 at 5M.
Design
Clean, deduplicated, modern text (encyclopaedic, educational web, books with front matter and hard line wraps removed), 10-15% conversation cut into window-sized exchanges, 10-20% evidence pairs (a related passage from another document, then the target), 5-10% worked solutions in plain arithmetic. Training units sized to the context window so no exchange or evidence pair is cut. A document-separator token.
Why it makes the model smarter
Most models learn to use retrieved text only in a late fine-tuning stage, if at all. Training the core on evidence windows from the start makes reading what it is handed a native skill, which is the skill Idea 1 depends on.

Idea 3. Exact tools during generation, with an answer check

What we measured
When the model writes "a op b =", a calculator writes the result: every arithmetic step is then exact. The answer check (the final answer must be a value the tool computed) confirmed 424 and replaced 1,188 stated answers in one run.
Design
A small set of deterministic tools invoked by patterns in the generated text: calculator, unit conversion, date arithmetic, later a sandboxed code runner and a memory lookup. Their output is written into the reply and the model continues from it; a check stage verifies final answers against tool results and the memory.
Why it makes the model smaller and more reliable
A network that does not have to learn exact arithmetic spends no capacity on it and never gets it subtly wrong. What remains for the network is deciding which operation to apply, which Idea 4 trains.

Idea 4. Reasoning by practice with a verifier

What we measured
Verified self-training on GSM8K at 5M: the model solves each training problem four times with its own stored solution hidden, keeps only answers the checker confirms, and is fine-tuned on them. New problems solved: 2.5% → 3.5% → 4.0% in two rounds; answers confirmed by the calculator +22%; training problems solved at least once 797 → 2,395.
Design
Repeated rounds of sample, verify, train on every task with an exact checker: arithmetic (calculator), code (tests), questions whose answer is in memory (lookup), format tasks (rules). Later, a reward-weighted variant (the reinforcement learning that produced today's reasoning models) once the simple loop plateaus.
Why it makes the model smarter
It is the method behind current reasoning models, needs no human labels, and plays to a small model's strength: it is cheap enough to sample many attempts.

Idea 5. Fine-tuning that keeps what pre-training built, and skills as adapters

What we measured
A Q&A stage at the pre-training learning rate erased every stage-1 data gain. The kept recipe: core at 1e-4 and new heads at 1e-3, warm-up and cosine decay, loss on the answer only, an end-of-answer token trained in the loss (without it answers ran on into invented text), and 30% of batches from pre-training text. Results: network alone 0.10 → 0.24 F1, stored answers 0.66 → 0.89.
Design
That recipe for every fine-tune, and beyond it: freeze the core and attach one small adapter per skill (about 1-5% of the core, LoRA-style), chosen per question by a router. Adapters are separate files, versioned and removable.
Why it makes the model compact
One core plus tiny skill files replaces several fine-tuned copies; a frozen core cannot be overwritten, so adding a skill never costs an old one.

Idea 6. Fast decoding: a KV cache, and drafts from copied spans

What we know
Our generator recomputes the whole window for every new token, which is slow (5M: 0.1-2 s per reply on one CPU core). When the model copies a retrieved answer, the next several tokens are almost certain.
Design
A key-value cache (keep each layer's attention keys and values for the tokens already processed, so each new token costs one step instead of a full window). Then speculative drafting: the copy head proposes the next 4-8 tokens from the retrieved passage, and the network checks all of them in a single pass, keeping the agreed prefix.
Expected effect
The cache: about 10-50 times faster generation at windows of 512-1,024 (standard, safe). Drafting: published retrieval-based drafting methods report 2-3 times more; copying from memory is its best case. Neither changes what the model says.

4. Build plan, in order

T1
KV cache in the generator (Idea 6, first half). Makes every following stage faster.
Pass: identical replies to the current generator on the chat probe; 10x faster or more at a 1,024 window.
T2
50M baseline of the full recipe (Ideas 1, 2, 5): the 50M core already trained on the P50 mix, its Q&A stage (running), report card and chat probe.
Pass: beats the 5M best on new and reworded questions and on the network-alone floor, not only on stored ones.
T3
Tools and checks broadened (Idea 3): units, dates, a code runner; checks against memory.
Pass: tool-checkable questions in the probe answered correctly at least 80%.
T4
Verified practice on the 50M core (Idea 4): three rounds on GSM8K and SVAMP.
Pass: new GSM8K problems at least double the 50M starting point.
T5
First skill adapter (Idea 5): an arithmetic adapter on the frozen core, against the full fine-tune.
Pass: matches or beats the full fine-tune on math with the conversation probe unchanged; under 5% of core size.
T6
Dense question key (Idea 1): trained on in-domain rewordings, combined with the keyword key.
Pass: reworded and synonym probe sections at least 4/5 each.
T7
Speculative drafting from copied spans (Idea 6, second half).
Pass: identical replies; at least 2x faster on answers copied from memory.
T8
125M Traditional Hybrid on about 10 GB of the same mix, with every stage above.
Pass: on knowledge it holds, within five points of a conventional model several times larger; reasoning measured on held-out GSM8K and SVAMP.

5. What we leave out, and why

Idea from the researchWhy it is not in this plan
Aggressive compression of the input (phrase codes, packing more text per token)Shorter sequences made the model 2.5-4% worse: a lossless encoder cannot add information, and rarer codes are harder to learn. A standard tokenizer is fine.
Count tables mixed into the output+10% at 0.8M, nothing at 4.7M, worse at 19M. A larger core learns these statistics itself.
Our own compiler in place of a standard tokenizerEqual to a standard tokenizer within 1%; its value was stable keys for memory, which any deterministic tokenizer plus a keyword index also gives.
Bigger core as the first leverData and recipe gave the large gains first; size pays once those are right (50M: -27% to -55% error on the same tests).
Paragraph-aligned training windows for proseRandom windows gave slightly better fluency; alignment helps only structured units, which this plan keeps whole by sizing them to the window.

7. What this plan is, in one line

A conventional model, except that facts sit in an index it reads, exact work is done by tools it calls, skills are small adapters it loads, and reasoning is practised against a checker, so the network itself can be small and fast while the system as a whole answers like a much larger one on the tasks it covers.
